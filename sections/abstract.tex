\section*{Abstract}

Early detection of plant diseases is an important challenge in both agricultural and forested ecosystems, where delayed identification can cause significant ecological and economic damage. This project investigates whether large-scale vegetation anomalies observable in historical remote sensing data precede documented plant disease outbreaks, focusing on the Mountain Pine Beetle (MPB) infestation in Boulder County, Colorado.

We construct a scalable, cloud-native Big Data pipeline that ingests satellite imagery as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs (COGs) on Amazon S3, indexes spatiotemporal metadata in a PostGIS database with GIST spatial indexing, and processes pixel-level spectral data using Apache PySpark. Three distinct real-world datasets are analyzed independently through the same pipeline: a 2015--2026 archive at 50\,m resolution (3.6\,GB, 464 objects), a 2024--2026 archive at 30\,m resolution (2.8\,GB, 96 objects), and a 2019--2026 archive at 20\,m resolution (15\,GB, 150 images).

For each dataset, ESA WorldCover land cover data isolates forested pixels, NDVI and NDMI vegetation indices are computed in PySpark, and anomalies are flagged via Z-score comparison against a per-pixel historical baseline. Evaluation demonstrates that GIST-indexed queries reduce latency by 15--25$\times$ over sequential scans, that PySpark throughput scales approximately linearly with data volume, and that the Z-score detector achieves approximately 71\% recall in identifying vegetation stress events corresponding to periods of documented MPB activity.

The identical pipeline processing all three datasets validates the system's architectural scalability across resolution, volume, and temporal range.
